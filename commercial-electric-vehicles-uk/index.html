Electric van grants, ZEV mandate, tax and rules in the UK

Updated 10 October 2026. Every figure links to its source. Rules and grant rates change, so check them before you buy. How we check facts.

The grants, tax and rules that apply to commercial EVs in the UK, with a focus on electric vans.

Quick answer

  • Van grant: up to £2,500 off a small electric van and up to £5,000 off a large one, under the Zero Emission Van Grant, which runs until March 2030. The dealer takes it off the price. [23] [24]
  • ZEV mandate: 24% of each manufacturer's new van sales must be zero-emission in 2026, rising to 70% in 2030 and 100% in 2035. Battery-electric vans were 10.8% of sales from January to September 2026. [3] [17]
  • Under review: the government is consulting until 23 October 2026 on cutting the 2030 van target to 60%, 50% or 40%, or keeping 70% with longer flexibilities. [20]
  • Licence: a car licence covers electric vans up to 4.25 tonnes. [7]
  • Tax: no van benefit charge on zero-emission company vans in 2026/27, but the same vehicle tax as diesel since April 2025. [8] [9]

How many electric vans are sold in the UK?

  • 2025: a record 30,169 battery-electric vans were registered, up 36.2% on 2024. That was 9.5% of the new van market, short of the 16% ZEV mandate target (SMMT figures, battery-electric vans only; the DfT counts 9.3% across all zero-emission light goods vehicles). [1]
  • 2026 so far: battery-electric vans took 10.8% of new van registrations from January to September, against this year's 24% target. September set a monthly record of 4,832 electric vans (9.6% of that month's sales), while August had the highest monthly share so far, 16.3%. [2] [17]

What is the UK ZEV mandate?

The Zero Emission Vehicle (ZEV) mandate is the UK rule that requires every car and van manufacturer to make a rising share of its new UK sales zero-emission each year, reaching 100% in 2035. [3] [14]

  • What happens if a manufacturer misses its target: it pays a fine for each vehicle it falls short, cut in April 2025 to £12,000 per car and £15,000 per van (from £15,000 and £18,000). [4]
  • Flexibilities: manufacturers can bank credits from good years, borrow from future years (now allowed until 2029), buy credits from other manufacturers, and swap between car and van credits. [4]
  • Petrol, diesel and hybrids: new pure petrol and diesel cars can be sold until 2030 and hybrid cars until 2035. New petrol, diesel and hybrid vans can all be sold until 2035. [4]
  • How it's going: battery-electric vans were 9.5% of new van sales in 2025, against a 16% target. Battery-electric cars took 26.2% of new car sales in the first nine months of 2026, against 33%. Because of the flexibilities, missing the headline share doesn't automatically mean fines. [1] [15]
  • Under review: the government launched a review of the mandate on 14 August 2026, and its consultation closes on 23 October 2026. It covers the yearly targets and the flexibilities. [16] See what's proposed for vans.

ZEV mandate review 2026: what's proposed for vans

The government is consulting on four options for the van targets. It opened the consultation on 14 August 2026 with the Scottish and Welsh governments and Northern Ireland's Department for Infrastructure, and it closes on 23 October 2026. Nothing has been decided yet: the government will publish its response after the consultation ends. [20]

Options for the 2030 van target

Current rules70%Flexibilities such as banking and borrowing credits end in 2029

  • What isn't changing: every option keeps the 100% target for 2035, so new petrol, diesel and hybrid vans still end that year. The 2030 end of new petrol and diesel cars stays too. [16] [20]
  • Why it's being reviewed: the Department for Transport's own forecast has electric sales in 2030 falling short of the 70% van and 80% car targets. Battery-electric vans were 10.8% of new van sales from January to September 2026, against this year's 24% target. [17] [21]
  • Flexibilities: the consultation also asks whether the flexibilities should continue beyond 2029 if the headline targets are cut. [20]
  • Cars: there are four separate options for the car targets.
  • How to respond: anyone can respond online through the consultation page on GOV.UK before it closes on 23 October 2026. [20]

  • How long it runs: the plug-in van grant, extended in August 2025, was replaced from 1 April 2026 by the Zero Emission Van Grant. It runs until 31 March 2030 or until its budget runs out. The rates above apply in 2026/27 and can change in later years, so check GOV.UK before you order. [18] [23] [24]
  • Which vans qualify: new vans on the government's approved list with zero tailpipe emissions that can cover at least 60 miles without emitting CO2. [19] [24]
  • Limit: each business, organisation or individual can get up to 1,500 van grants a financial year. The limit applies to the end customer, not the leasing company. [25]

Can I drive an electric van on a car licence?

Yes, up to 4.25 tonnes. A standard category B (car) licence normally covers vans up to 3.5 tonnes. Since 10 June 2025, it also covers zero-emission vans up to 4.25 tonnes, and the five hours of extra training previously required has been dropped. Electric vans need the extra allowance because their batteries add weight that would otherwise eat into payload. [7]

How are electric vans taxed?

  • Van benefit charge: when an employee can use a company van privately, they pay income tax on a flat taxable value. For 2026/27 that value is £4,170 for diesel and petrol vans and £0 for zero-emission vans. It doesn't apply to self-employed drivers. [8]
  • Vehicle tax (VED): since 1 April 2025, electric vans pay vehicle tax at the same rate as petrol and diesel vans. [9]
  • Chargepoints: businesses can claim a 100% first-year allowance on new EV chargepoints until 31 March 2027 (Corporation Tax) or 5 April 2027 (Income Tax). [10]
  • Pay-per-mile tax: Electric Vehicle Excise Duty, a charge of 3p a mile for electric cars from April 2028, won't apply to vans when it starts. [26]

Do electric vans pay the Congestion Charge, ULEZ or Clean Air Zone charges?

  • London Congestion Charge: the full exemption for electric vehicles ended on 25 December 2025. From 2 January 2026 the daily charge is £18, and electric vans registered for Auto Pay get a 50% discount, so they pay £9. The van discount falls to 25% from March 2030. [11]
  • ULEZ and Clean Air Zones: these charge vehicles that don't meet emission standards. Fully electric vans meet them, so they don't pay. See every zone's charges for vans.

What charging grants are there for businesses?

  • Depot Charging Scheme: for businesses running (or planning) electric vans, HGVs or coaches. For 2026/27 projects it pays 70% of chargepoint and installation costs, up to £1 million per organisation. The next application window opens on 28 October 2026 and closes on 29 January 2027, for projects starting from April 2027; its grant rate hasn't been confirmed yet. [12]
  • Workplace Charging Scheme: £500 per socket from 1 April 2026, for up to 40 sockets. It runs until 31 March 2027, which the government has said will be the final year. [13]

Over a dozen, from small vans like the Ford E-Transit Courier to large ones like the Renault Master E-Tech.
