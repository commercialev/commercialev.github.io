How many UK vans are still diesel?

At the end of 2025 there were 4.88 million vans and other light goods vehicles licensed in the UK. Only 113,000 of them (2.3%) were zero-emission. Almost all the rest run on diesel. [1]

New vans are changing faster: 9.3% of the 330,000 light goods vehicles registered in 2025 were zero-emission. [1]

When will diesel vans be banned?

  • New diesel vans can be sold until 2035. In April 2025 the government confirmed that new petrol, diesel and hybrid vans can still be sold until then. [2]
  • The 2035 date applies to new sales. Diesel vans already on the road can still be driven, and bought and sold second-hand.
  • Before then, manufacturers have targets. The ZEV mandate requires 24% of each manufacturer's new van sales to be zero-emission in 2026, rising to 70% in 2030. See the full table.

Is an electric van cheaper to run than a diesel?

Usually, if you can charge at home or at a depot. Diesel reached a record UK average of 199.18p a litre on 28 September 2026, after attacks on Middle East oil supply cut diesel flows. [3]

Diesel199.18p a litre [3]23p£2,264

Assumes a diesel van doing 40 miles per gallon and an electric van doing 3 miles per kWh. Home rate is the household price cap for October to December 2026, which has no VAT on electricity until March 2027. Business electricity tariffs differ. To use your own figures: diesel pence per mile = price per litre × 4.546 ÷ mpg; electric pence per mile = price per kWh ÷ miles per kWh.

The table shows the catch: a van that relies on rapid public chargers can cost more per mile than diesel. The savings come from cheaper overnight charging.

Where do diesel vans pay to drive?

  • London ULEZ: covers all London boroughs. Diesel vans up to 3.5 tonnes that don't meet the Euro 6 standard pay £12.50 a day. Most diesel vans sold since September 2016 meet Euro 6; check yours with TfL's vehicle checker. [6]
  • Clean Air Zones: Bath, Birmingham, Bradford, Bristol, Sheffield, and Newcastle and Gateshead charge vans that don't meet Euro 6 (diesel) or Euro 4 (petrol). Portsmouth's zone doesn't charge vans. Daily charges vary by city. [7]
  • Electric vans meet all of these standards, so they don't pay ULEZ or Clean Air Zone charges. They do now pay a discounted London Congestion Charge.

Is an electric van right for your work?

  • Where the van parks overnight: home or depot charging is what makes the running costs work.
  • Daily mileage: compare your longest regular day with the van's real-world range, not just the official figure, and allow for winter.
  • Payload: batteries add weight. Since June 2025, a car licence covers zero-emission vans up to 4.25 tonnes, which helps recover payload. More on the licence rule.
  • Where you drive: regular trips into London or a Clean Air Zone in an older diesel add up quickly.
