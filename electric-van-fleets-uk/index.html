The UK's biggest electric van fleets, and the vans they use

Updated 7 October 2026. Based on companies' public announcements and trade press reports, each linked below. Inclusion doesn't imply any endorsement. How we check facts.

Quick answer

  • Royal Mail runs the biggest electric van fleet reported in the UK: its 9,000th electric van went into service in August 2026. [1]
  • Openreach and DPD each run thousands of electric vans, Mitie runs over 6,000 EVs including cars, and Tesco passed 2,000 electric home delivery vans in September 2026.
  • Most-used brands: big fleets mix makes, but Peugeot, Vauxhall, Ford, Mercedes-Benz, Volkswagen and Maxus come up most often.

Which companies use which electric van brands?

  • Ford: Openreach (E-Transit Custom) and DPD (E-Transit). [3] [10]
  • Maxus: Tesco (eDeliver 9) and DPD. [11] [9]
  • Mercedes-Benz: DPD and Amazon (eSprinter), plus Amazon's eVito. [9] [13]
  • Peugeot: Royal Mail (e-Partner and e-Expert, many built at Ellesmere Port in Cheshire). [2]
  • Toyota and Renault: Openreach. [4] [5]
  • Vauxhall: Openreach (Vivaro Electric and Combo Electric), British Gas (Vivaro-e) and Mitie. [5] [14] [8]
  • Volkswagen: Mitie (ID. Buzz Cargo). [7]

For range, payload and prices of these models, see electric vans compared.

What do the biggest electric van fleets have in common?

  • They buy from several makers. Openreach's 2025 order alone was split across Ford, Stellantis (Vauxhall), Toyota and Renault, matching different vans to different jobs. [4]
  • They switch in stages. Royal Mail ordered its first 100 electric vans in 2017 and reached 9,000 in 2026; about a third of its delivery routes are now fully electric. [15] [1]
  • They charge at their own sites. Depot charging is what makes the running costs work at scale. What a depot charger costs.
  • They pick vans that match their diesel payloads. DPD says its eSprinters carry the same payload as the 3.5-tonne diesel vans they replace. [9]
