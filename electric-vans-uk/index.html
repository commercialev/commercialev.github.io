Electric vans in the UK: range, payload and price compared

Updated 5 October 2026. Every figure links to its source. Specifications vary by version, so check the exact van with the dealer. How we check facts.

Quick answer

  • Longest range: Renault Master E-Tech, up to 285 miles (official figure). [7]
  • Most payload: Renault Master E-Tech, up to 1,625kg; Ford E-Transit van, up to 1,460kg. [7] [8]
  • Lowest price after the grant: Kia PV5 Cargo, from £22,645 plus VAT. [15]
  • Battery warranty: usually 8 years or 100,000 miles. [12]
  • Real-world range: plan on 60–70% of the official figure for a loaded van in winter. [13]

Which electric van has the longest range?

The Renault Master E-Tech, at up to 285 miles on the official test, followed by the Volkswagen ID. Buzz Cargo (277 miles) and the Mercedes-Benz eSprinter (277 miles). The longest-range medium vans are the ID. Buzz Cargo and the Kia PV5 Cargo (258 miles). [5] [6] [7] [9]

Which electric van carries the most?

Among panel vans, the Renault Master E-Tech (up to 1,625kg), the Vauxhall Movano Electric and its twins in 4.25-tonne form (up to 1,480kg) and the Ford E-Transit (up to 1,460kg). The heaviest-payload medium van is the Vauxhall Vivaro Electric and its twins (up to 1,226kg), followed by the Ford E-Transit Custom (up to 1,088kg). [7] [8] [10] [11] [4]

How much does an electric van cost?

Launch list prices, excluding VAT:

  • Ford E-Transit Courier: from £27,000 before its £2,500 small-van grant, so about £24,500 after it. [1] [16]
  • Kia PV5 Cargo: from £27,645 with the standard battery or £30,145 with the long-range battery, before the £5,000 grant. That makes it the cheapest electric van after the grant, from £22,645. [15]
  • Volkswagen ID. Buzz Cargo: from £34,460. [5]
  • Renault Master E-Tech: from £37,500, including the grant. [7]
  • Farizon SV: from £45,000, rising to £56,000. [14]

Dealers often discount electric vans heavily, so these are starting points. The plug-in van grant takes up to £2,500 off small vans and £5,000 off larger ones, and the dealer applies it for you.

How far will an electric van really go?

Less than the official figure. Fleet tests suggest a fully loaded van in a British winter gets 60–70% of its official range. A van rated at 200 miles is safer planned at about 120–140 miles, especially if it can't be pre-heated while still plugged in. [13]

How long do electric van batteries last?

Most manufacturers cover the battery for 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first; Ford, Nissan and Volkswagen all do. Volkswagen's warranty promises the battery keeps at least 70% of its original capacity over that period. Check the terms for the exact van, especially if you're buying used. [12]
